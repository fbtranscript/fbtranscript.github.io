How to Get Captions on a Facebook Video

Turn on captions for any Facebook video and read along as it plays.

Captions make Facebook videos easier to follow, whether you are watching without sound, learning a language, or hard of hearing. Facebook generates captions for many videos automatically, and creators can also upload their own. This guide shows how to turn captions on, where to find them, and what to do when they are missing.

Turn captions on while watching

Tap or click the CC button on the video player to enable captions. On mobile, the button appears when you tap the video; on desktop, it sits in the player controls. If the video has captions, they appear at the bottom of the picture as it plays. Tap CC again to turn them off.

Turn captions on in settings

In Facebook settings, look for Accessibility or Video settings and enable captions by default. This means every video that has captions will show them automatically. You can also choose the caption style and size in some browsers and on some phones.

When captions are missing

Not every video has captions. Automatic captions depend on the audio being clear and in a supported language, and creators can choose not to add them. If a video has no captions, try the transcript option described below, or ask the creator to add captions to the video.

Captions for creators

If you upload videos, add captions to reach more people. Facebook's automatic captions are a good start, but review them for errors, especially with names and technical terms. Uploading an SRT file gives you full control over the wording and timing.

Putting it into practice

Start with a single, short example and work through the steps above from beginning to end. Do not try to do everything at once, and do not use an important or irreplaceable video for your first attempt. Once the first try works, repeat the process with a second example, and you will find the steps become familiar quickly. Keep your files organised as you go, give them clear names, and check the finished result on the device where you plan to use it, whether that is your phone, your computer, or a tablet.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is rushing: pasting the wrong link, picking the lowest quality, or downloading from the first site that appears in a search without checking whether it is trustworthy. The second is ignoring storage and file names, which leaves you with a folder full of files called download-1 and download-2 that you cannot tell apart. The third is forgetting the creator: always credit the person who made the original, keep personal copies personal, and ask before you reuse someone else's work in public.
