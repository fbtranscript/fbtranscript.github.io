Facebook Video ID Explained: How to Find It

What a Facebook video ID is, where to find it in a URL, and why it matters.

Every Facebook video has a unique ID, a long number that identifies it in Facebook's system. You need the ID for embedding, for some tools, for reporting a video, and for finding its caption file. This guide explains where the ID lives in different kinds of Facebook URLs, and how to extract it reliably.

IDs in standard video URLs

A typical Facebook video URL looks like facebook.com/watch/?v=1234567890, and the number after v= is the video ID. In a post URL, the ID may appear after /videos/ or in a long string of numbers. Copy the full URL first, so you do not lose a digit.

IDs in short and shared links

Shared links may be shortened, such as fb.watch/abc123, and they do not show the ID directly. Open the short link in your browser and let it redirect to the full URL, where the ID appears. Do not guess the ID from a short link, because the short code is not the same number.

IDs on mobile

On the Facebook app, tap Share, then Copy Link, to get the video URL, then paste it somewhere you can read it. The ID is in the URL in the same way as on desktop. If the link is a short fb.watch link, open it in a browser to expand it first.

Why the ID matters

Tools that fetch captions, embed a video, or analyse a post usually ask for the video ID rather than the full URL. The ID is also useful when you report a problem, because support can find the exact video. Keep the ID with the video title in your notes so you can find the video again later.

Putting it into practice

Start with a single, short example and work through the steps above from beginning to end. Do not try to do everything at once, and do not use an important or irreplaceable video for your first attempt. Once the first try works, repeat the process with a second example, and you will find the steps become familiar quickly. Keep your files organised as you go, give them clear names, and check the finished result on the device where you plan to use it, whether that is your phone, your computer, or a tablet.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is rushing: pasting the wrong link, picking the lowest quality, or downloading from the first site that appears in a search without checking whether it is trustworthy. The second is ignoring storage and file names, which leaves you with a folder full of files called download-1 and download-2 that you cannot tell apart. The third is forgetting the creator: always credit the person who made the original, keep personal copies personal, and ask before you reuse someone else's work in public.
