Facebook Video Transcripts and Privacy

What happens to your video and transcript when you use a transcription tool.

Uploading a video or its audio to a transcription service means sharing it with a third party, and that has privacy implications. This guide explains what to check before you upload, how to protect sensitive content, and when to transcribe locally instead of in the cloud.

Read the privacy policy

Before you upload, check what the service does with your audio and transcript. Some services delete your files after transcription, while others keep them to improve their models. Look for a clear statement about retention, and avoid services that do not explain what happens to your data.

Avoid uploading sensitive content

Do not upload videos that contain personal information, private conversations, or confidential material to a free online tool unless you trust it. For sensitive content, transcribe locally with software on your own computer, or type the transcript yourself. This keeps the audio on your device.

Check who can see the transcript

If you publish a transcript, remember that it can be searched and copied. Do not publish a transcript of a private video, and remove personal details such as phone numbers and addresses before you share it. If the video is public, the transcript is effectively public too.

Delete files when you are done

After transcription, delete the uploaded audio and the transcript from the service if it lets you. Keep your own copy in a secure place, and do not leave the file in a shared folder. Good data hygiene is simple: upload only what you need, and remove it when you are done.

Putting it into practice

Start with a single, short example and work through the steps above from beginning to end. Do not try to do everything at once, and do not use an important or irreplaceable video for your first attempt. Once the first try works, repeat the process with a second example, and you will find the steps become familiar quickly. Keep your files organised as you go, give them clear names, and check the finished result on the device where you plan to use it, whether that is your phone, your computer, or a tablet.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is rushing: pasting the wrong link, picking the lowest quality, or downloading from the first site that appears in a search without checking whether it is trustworthy. The second is ignoring storage and file names, which leaves you with a folder full of files called download-1 and download-2 that you cannot tell apart. The third is forgetting the creator: always credit the person who made the original, keep personal copies personal, and ask before you reuse someone else's work in public.
