Facebook Auto-Captions: How Accurate Are They?

What Facebook's automatic captions get right, what they get wrong, and how to fix them.

Facebook's automatic captions are generated by speech recognition, and they are good enough to follow most videos, but they are not perfect. They struggle with accents, background noise, names, and technical words. This guide explains how accurate they are, when to trust them, and how creators can improve them.

How accurate are they

For clear, slow speech in a quiet room, automatic captions are often 90 percent accurate or better. For fast speech, strong accents, music, or overlapping voices, accuracy drops sharply. A single wrong word can change the meaning of a sentence, so do not quote auto-captions without checking them.

Common mistakes

Auto-captions often confuse similar-sounding words, miss short words such as not, and fail on proper names. They also struggle with numbers, dates, and brand names. Punctuation is usually missing or guessed, so a long run of words can be hard to parse.

How creators can improve them

Creators should review auto-captions before publishing, fix errors in the caption editor, and upload a corrected SRT file when possible. Speaking clearly, reducing background noise, and avoiding talking over music all help the recogniser. A short delay before publishing gives time to check the captions.

How viewers can cope

If captions look wrong, listen to the audio as well, and use the context to work out the intended words. You can also slow the video down, which sometimes helps. If the captions are badly wrong, let the creator know, because they may not have checked them.

Putting it into practice

Start with a single, short example and work through the steps above from beginning to end. Do not try to do everything at once, and do not use an important or irreplaceable video for your first attempt. Once the first try works, repeat the process with a second example, and you will find the steps become familiar quickly. Keep your files organised as you go, give them clear names, and check the finished result on the device where you plan to use it, whether that is your phone, your computer, or a tablet.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is rushing: pasting the wrong link, picking the lowest quality, or downloading from the first site that appears in a search without checking whether it is trustworthy. The second is ignoring storage and file names, which leaves you with a folder full of files called download-1 and download-2 that you cannot tell apart. The third is forgetting the creator: always credit the person who made the original, keep personal copies personal, and ask before you reuse someone else's work in public.
