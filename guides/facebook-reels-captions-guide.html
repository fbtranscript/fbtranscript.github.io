Facebook Reels Captions: A Complete Guide

Captions for Facebook Reels: turning them on, adding them, and fixing them.

Reels are short, fast, and often watched without sound, which makes captions especially important. A Reel without captions can lose viewers in the first second, because they cannot tell what it is about. This guide covers captions for Reels from both sides: watching them, and adding them as a creator.

Turn captions on for Reels

Tap the CC button on a Reel to show captions, if the Reel has them. On the Facebook app, captions appear at the bottom of the screen. If the button is missing, the Reel has no captions, and you will need to listen to the audio or read the description instead.

Add captions when you create a Reel

When you upload a Reel, Facebook may generate captions automatically. Review them before you publish, because short, fast speech is easy for the recogniser to get wrong. You can also add text overlays in the editor, which work like captions and are visible even when captions are off.

Use text overlays well

Text overlays are a powerful tool for Reels, because they are part of the video itself. Keep them short, large, and high contrast, and place them where they do not cover your face or key visuals. Use them for the main point, and let the captions carry the full wording.

Fix caption errors on a Reel

If you spot an error after publishing, you can edit the Reel's captions in the video settings, or delete and re-upload the Reel with corrected captions. For a short Reel, re-uploading is often quicker. Check the captions on a phone, not just on desktop, because that is how most viewers will see them.

Putting it into practice

Start with a single, short example and work through the steps above from beginning to end. Do not try to do everything at once, and do not use an important or irreplaceable video for your first attempt. Once the first try works, repeat the process with a second example, and you will find the steps become familiar quickly. Keep your files organised as you go, give them clear names, and check the finished result on the device where you plan to use it, whether that is your phone, your computer, or a tablet.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is rushing: pasting the wrong link, picking the lowest quality, or downloading from the first site that appears in a search without checking whether it is trustworthy. The second is ignoring storage and file names, which leaves you with a folder full of files called download-1 and download-2 that you cannot tell apart. The third is forgetting the creator: always credit the person who made the original, keep personal copies personal, and ask before you reuse someone else's work in public.
